# The Mission

A [[Mission]] has one or more [[Objective|Objectives]] that must be reached and achieved. It consists of several [[Sector|Sectors]] linked together.

A Mission starts at a **Landing Zone (LZ)**, the position from which the [[Squad]] deploys. Some Missions offer a choice of several. Despite the name, not all missions start by aerial deployment.

Depending on the Mission Type, the Mission ends when the Objective is achieved, or when the Squad returns to the LZ, or when it reaches a separate **Extraction Zone (EZ)**.

## Mission Objective

Depending on the type of mission, there can be one or multiple objectives, which can be independent from each other or conditional. Objectives can require specific gear or impose specific parameters (for instance, demolition charges to take down a bridge).

## Mission Types

### Assault

Move from LZ to Objective. Take ground, break resistance, and finish the job. The mission usually ends once the Objective is achieved and the Squad controls the position.

### Raid

Move from LZ to Objective, complete the task, then exfiltrate. The mission requires an extraction route, either back to the LZ or toward a separate EZ.

### Sweep

Move through the mission area and hit targets of opportunity. A Sweep can have multiple Objectives, or require a set number of Objectives completed before extraction.

### Defense

Hold a position and prevent enemy success. The Squad starts at or near the Objective. Depending on the situation, the mission may involve fallback positions, relief, evacuation, or retaking lost ground.

| Mission Type | Objectives                                     |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Assault**  | Capture, Destroy, Neutralize                   |
| **Raid**     | Destroy, Extract, Recover, Disrupt, Neutralize |
| **Sweep**    | Destroy, Disrupt, Neutralize                   |
| **Defense**  | Hold                                           |

## Objectives

Pick or roll an Objective linked by the Mission Type, then roll on that verb's table.

### Capture

Take control of the Objective. It must remain usable afterward.

| 1d6  | Objective                                      |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Enemy bunker                                   |
| 2    | Weapons depot                                  |
| 3    | Chokepoint                                     |
| 4    | Command post                                   |
| 5    | Bridge, gate, or crossing                      |
| 6    | Sensor tower, signal station, or lookout point |

### Destroy

Remove the Objective from the battlefield. Collateral damage is expected.

| 1d6  | Objective                      |
| ---- | ------------------------------ |
| 1    | Ammunition cache               |
| 2    | Fuel dump or power plant       |
| 3    | Artillery position             |
| 4    | Armored vehicle or war machine |
| 5    | Communications array           |
| 6    | Fortified emplacement          |

### Neutralize

Make the Objective stop mattering. Killing it, disabling it, routing it, jamming it, or forcing it to withdraw all count.

| 1d6  | Objective                                         |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Enemy officer or command cadre                    |
| 2    | Heavy weapon team                                 |
| 3    | Patrol group or hunter-killer squad               |
| 4    | Sniper, spotter, or forward observer              |
| 5    | Elite guard unit                                  |
| 6    | Drone, beast, machine, or other battlefield asset |

### Extract

Get the Objective out alive. The Objective may be fragile, unwilling, wounded, or hunted.

| 1d6  | Objective                       |
| ---- | -------------------------------- |
| 1    | Wounded officer                 |
| 2    | Pinned-down friendly squad      |
| 3    | Intelligence asset or informant |
| 4    | Prisoner of war                 |
| 5    | Civilian specialist             |
| 6    | Defector, traitor, or turncoat  |

### Recover

Bring the Objective back. It may be portable, buried, locked down, booby-trapped, or too heavy to move without effort.

| 1d6  | Objective                                 |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------ |
| 1    | Black box, data core, or mission recorder |
| 2    | Experimental weapon                       |
| 3    | Lost banner, relic, or regimental symbol  |
| 4    | Classified maps or targeting data         |
| 5    | Medical supplies or survival gear         |
| 6    | Crashed drone, probe, or scout vehicle    |

### Disrupt

Throw the enemy's plan into chaos. The Objective does not need to be permanently destroyed.

| 1d6  | Objective                                               |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Supply route                                            |
| 2    | Reinforcement column                                    |
| 3    | Communications network                                  |
| 4    | Enemy mustering point                                   |
| 5    | Targeting system or fire-control link                   |
| 6    | Ritual, broadcast, launch sequence, or command protocol |

### Hold

Keep control until relief, evacuation, dawn, extraction, or the enemy breaks.

| 1d6  | Objective                          |
| ---- | ----------------------------------- |
| 1    | Forward outpost                    |
| 2    | Evacuation zone                    |
| 3    | Breached wall or open gate         |
| 4    | Bridge, road, or landing pad       |
| 5    | Civilian shelter or field hospital |
| 6    | Fallen command post                |

## Area of Operations

Take a moment to establish the general landscape of the mission. Where does it take place: a ruined city, a mountain pass, a tropical island, a (space)ship under boarding action?

Then, place the starting location (LZ) and Objective(s). The space between these locations become [[Sector|Sectors]]: the zones that the Squad will need to traverse. There's multiple ways to approach this: linear, branching paths, hexcrawls, pointcrawls, free movement on an existing map.

## Difficulty & Threat Level

> *"Actual to Squad, listen up, quick and clean: you hit the LZ, move hard to Sector Alpha. Clear light resistance, no prolonged fights - keep momentum. Push through to Bravo, expect open ground; use smoke for crossing and suppressing fire from two-man element. Charlie is the objective. Take it, dig in, hold until evac or relief. Holdout timer starts on contact. Rules of engagement: no unnecessary heroics, conserve ammo. Questions? No? Stack up. Move at 30, eyes sharp. Good hunting."*

A Mission has a set **Difficulty**, which determines the maximum Threat Level of foes encountered. Assign a difficulty that makes sense, or roll {roll:1d6|Difficulty}:

| 1d6  | Difficulty | Max Threat Level | Notes                                       |
| ---- | ---------- | ----------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 1–3  | Routine    | 2 (Standard)     | Patrols, lightly contested                  |
| 4–5  | Hazardous  | 3 (Heavy)        | Standard operation into active battlefields |
| 6    | Desperate  | 4 (Overwhelming) | Intense opposition, grave danger            |

Threat Level affects the likelihood of a firefight starting in a favorable position, the difficulty of gaining [[Momentum]], risk of serious injury, and the likelihood of the enemy using battlefield-shifting tactics.

**Threat Level is relative to the Squad, not an objective measure of enemy strength.** DANGER CLOSE is built as an ode to the grunt, but the same numbers hold if the Squad is elite operatives or power-armored super soldiers: the fight around them scales to match.

- A TL 1 threat facing five regular infantry might be a handful of militia with outdated rifles.
- A TL 1 threat facing five gene-forged super soldiers might be an entire mechanized platoon that isn't equipped for what's coming.

The number is also silent on composition. A TL 4 force can be an overwhelming horde or a handful of elites who know exactly what they're doing. Threat Level sets how hard the fight is, not who or how many are in it.

## Airspace

**Airspace** is a descriptor for who currently holds air superiority. By default, this can be considered *contested*. In longer campaigns consisting of multiple missions, completing certain missions can affect the Airspace of missions in the future.

| Airspace  | Transport                  | [[Support]]   |
| --------- | --------------------------- | ------------- |
| Clear     | No hindrance               | No hindrance  |
| Contested | 2-in-6 odds of taking fire | No hindrance  |
| Hostile   | 4-in-6 odds of taking fire | Not available |

If flying in Contested or Hostile airspace, roll to see if the transport is targeted when moving to a mission and on extraction.

If a transport takes fire, roll {roll:1d6|Take fire}. On a **1-3**, the transport goes down. These odds may be modified by factors such as pilot skill or vehicle resilience (for example, a skilled pilot might make the odds 2-in-6 instead).

If the transport is shot down, each [[Trooper]] rolls {roll:1d6|Crash injury}. On a **1-2**, they suffer an [[Injury]] as the vehicle crashes.

- If en route to the LZ, the Squad lands off-course and must travel {roll:1d3|Extra sectors} extra Sectors on foot.
- If extracting, the Squad must hold off enemies at the crash site (a Defensive [[Engagement]] - survive for TL + {roll:1d2|Exchanges} [[Exchange|Exchanges]]) or reroute to a secondary Extraction Zone ({roll:1d3|Sectors away} Sectors away), or risk being left behind.
